#### 2. Conceptual Tensions and Contradictions
Several interesting tensions emerge across the notes, reflecting the practitioner's path as a householder and software engineer:

*   **Secular/Rational Practice vs. Ritual/Devotion**
    *   *Tension:* The author struggles with traditional devotional concepts like prayer and ritual, but practices mantra recitation and visualizes deities.
    *   *Examples:*
        *   In [Tschenresi Sadhana.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/Tschenresi%20Sadhana.md) (Line 293): *"Ah no, it's [[prayer]]. Well, I don't normally think I pray, but I do recitate mantras..."*
        *   In [gate gate paragate parasamgate bodhi svaha.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/gate%20gate%20paragate%20parasamgate%20bodhi%20svaha.md) (Line 867): *"Sometimes I say it while lighting a candle. I think it makes sense, although I try to remember it is best not to become attached to [[ritual]]."*
        *   In [buddha.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/buddha.md) (Line 555): *"Mostly [[rational]], but I think it's rational to be [[irrational]] some of the time (an example of a [[hedge]] maybe)."*
        *   In [dharma.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/dharma.md) (Line 716): The shift from *"secular buddhist"* to *"buddhist"* in 2022.
*   **Spiritual Value vs. Institutional Ethics (The Shambhala Problem)**
    *   *Tension:* Deep attraction to the teachings of Chögyam Trungpa and the "sacred path of the warrior," contrasted with profound concern over institutional power abuse and sexual misconduct within Shambhala.
    *   *Examples:*
        *   In [shambhala buddhism.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/shambhala%20buddhism.md) (Line 1468): *"I [[like]] it; might optimally represent me in some ways (...) although the power abuse and sexual misconduct accusations are very worrying."*
*   **Householder/Lay Engagement vs. Renunciation ("Burning Kindly" vs. "Gone")**
    *   *Tension:* The tension between full monastic renunciation (*"gone"* or *gate gate*) and a householder's life of active engagement, love, and passion in the world (*"burn kindly"*).
    *   *Examples:*
        *   In [maybe also.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/maybe%20also.md) (Line 1123): *"coming from [[gone]], one for the [[non strictly monastic]] life: [[Love]] Burn kindly! If you have to burn, [[burn kindly]]!"*
*   **The Psychological Toll of Knowing Suffering vs. The Duty to Address It (Info Hazards)**
    *   *Tension:* Deeply understanding systemic suffering (like dementia or coordination failure) can act as an emotional "info hazard," yet the Dharma dictates that suffering must be looked at directly.
    *   *Examples:*
        *   In [dementia.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/dementia.md) (Line 702): *"Could be seen as an [[info hazard]]... in the sense that learning more about the nature of dementia... can be deeply affecting. Then again, this is true for many cases of [[suffering]]... discussing the nature of suffering can be beneficial, and it is aligned with the [[Dharma]]..."*
*   **Opportunity Cost and Skilful Action in Complex Systems**
    *   *Tension:* The desire to act compassionately is bottlenecked by the extreme complexity of modern interdependent networks and the opportunity cost of choosing one action over another.
    *   *Examples:*
        *   In [Open Letter to Jigme Rinpoche.md](file:///home/flancian/garden/Open%20Letter%20to%20Jigme%20Rinpoche.md) (Line 217): *"When doing so, how can one maximize the chances of practicing skilfull/right action, given there are many possible ways to try to help, and the world is so complex and inter-dependent? ... How can one try to make the right choice, when there are so many options and a matching opportunity cost?"*
