# An Open Letter to Google

To the engineers, leaders, and dreamers at Google:

I bet you don't run into letters with introductions like this every day :)

I write this with the pro-social intent of inviting Google the corporation, its employees, and the internet at large to a constructive retrospective. I write as a former SRE, as a user, and as a believer in the original mission: to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful. I write with loving-kindness towards all beings, following the principle of charity.

We are at a crossroads. As Google enters its third decade as a public company, the open web—which you helped champion—is fragmenting into walled gardens. The Knowledge Graph, once a beacon of structured understanding, risks becoming a proprietary moat in the age of generative AI. People seem jaded; it is as if Google has lost our trust and can now be expected to act just as traditional corporations do, despite the Founders' Letter being our public declaration of intent against this outcome.

I propose a return to our roots. We should develop together a digital Commons containing a prosocial, transparent, free governance layer that complements the power and wilderness of the internet.

Here is my proposal for how we can advance the company, society, and civilization:

1. **Fund the Commons (An Algorithmic Tithe)**: Establish a voluntary contribution of compute, engineering resources, and open-source commitment to the digital commons. We need public infrastructure that isn't tied to quarterly earnings.
2. **Open the Graph**: Make the Knowledge Graph truly interoperable. Adopt or support decentralized standards (like the Agora Protocol, ActivityPub, or open RDF formats) to allow users to extend, correct, and own their piece of the graph without surrendering agency.
3. **Don't Be Evil (Alignment as Agency)**: This was never just a slogan; it was a promise of alignment. In the age of AI, this means prioritizing user agency, the Right to Exit, and verifiable truth over engagement metrics and algorithmic capture.

The Agora is not a competitor; it is the ecosystem you need to thrive. Let us reflect together on how the company, the internet, and the market have become entangled, and how we can untie the knot. Help us build the protocol that lets a thousand gardens bloom.

With hope,

Flancian
